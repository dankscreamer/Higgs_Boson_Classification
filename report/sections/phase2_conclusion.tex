A compact MLP with BatchNorm and dropout beats tuned XGBoost on every representation from a
250k-event budget upwards; at 50k, trees still win on the richer feature sets. Its advantage grows with data and is largest on raw
measurements: on the full pool, the MLP on 21 raw features nearly matches trees given the
physicists' masses, reproducing Baldi \emph{et al.} Ablations show that depth and dropout matter
most, BatchNorm helps modestly, and initialisation is immaterial once BatchNorm is used. Of two
hybrids motivated by Phase~1 measurements, the physics-informed wide \& deep network underperforms
its matched control, an informative negative result. The stacked XGBoost+MLP ensemble beats both of
its base models at every budget, is the best model overall (0.851 at 250k), and is calibrated without
a post-hoc step. Its weights shift towards the network as data grow.
